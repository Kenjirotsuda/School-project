EXHIBIT 01 / §1

THE TOOLS OF DISCOVERY

How do we know?

Science is not simply a collection of facts. It is a way to ask questions, test ideas, and revise explanations when the evidence changes.

01

Hypothesis

A testable proposed explanation or prediction. An investigation can gather evidence that supports it or shows it needs revision.

A TESTABLE IDEA
02

Scientific Theory

A broad explanation supported by many lines of evidence and repeated testing. Theories explain how or why natural phenomena happen.

AN EVIDENCE-BACKED EXPLANATION
03

Scientific Law

A concise description of a pattern in nature often expressed mathematically. Laws describe what happens under specified conditions.

A DESCRIBED PATTERN
✳

A theory does not “turn into” a law. Laws and theories serve different purposes: laws describe patterns, while theories explain them. Scientific conclusions remain open to refinement as new evidence appears.

EXHIBIT 02 / §2

COSMIC SPARK

How did the universe
begin to change?

The Big Bang model describes a universe that expanded from an extremely hot, dense early state. It was not an ordinary explosion into empty space: space itself has expanded.

EARLIEST MOMENTS

Rapid expansion

The universe was much hotter and denser than it is today. Our current physics does not yet provide a complete account of the very earliest instant.

FIRST MINUTES

Light nuclei form

As the universe cooled, conditions allowed mostly hydrogen nuclei, helium nuclei, and tiny amounts of other light nuclei to form.

ABOUT 380,000 YEARS

First free-traveling light

Atoms formed as the universe cooled enough for electrons to join nuclei. Light then traveled more freely; we observe that ancient light today as the cosmic microwave background.

HUNDREDS OF MILLIONS OF YEARS

Stars and galaxies

Gravity gathered matter into structures. The first stars ignited, and galaxies developed over cosmic time.

TODAY

An expanding cosmos

Galaxies on large scales are generally moving apart as space expands. The expansion is accelerating according to current observations.

THE EVIDENCE

Three clues
from the cosmos.

01

Expansion

Light from many distant galaxies is redshifted. On large scales, the pattern shows that the universe is expanding.

02

Cosmic microwave background

A faint microwave glow fills the sky. It is leftover radiation from the early universe, cooled by expansion.

03

Light elements

The observed abundance of light elements, especially hydrogen and helium, broadly matches predictions from the early universe.

The model is strongly supported by multiple independent observations; open questions remain about the earliest moments and the nature of dark matter and dark energy.

EXHIBIT 03 / §3

GRAVITY & MOTION

Why do planets
keep moving?

A planet’s forward motion and the Sun’s gravitational pull work together. The planet continually falls toward the Sun, but its sideways motion keeps it in orbit.

→
STARPLANETSIMPLIFIED ORBIT DIAGRAM

THE CENTRAL IDEA

An orbit is a balance
of motion and gravity.

Gravity changes a planet’s direction of travel. Because the planet already has motion across space, it follows a curved path instead of falling straight into the Sun.

This diagram is conceptual and not to scale. Real orbits vary in shape and size.

01

Kepler’s First Law

Planets move in elliptical orbits, with the Sun at one focus of each ellipse.

02

Kepler’s Second Law

A line from a planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times. A planet moves faster when closer to the Sun and slower when farther away.

03

Kepler’s Third Law

For objects orbiting the same star, a planet farther away generally takes longer to complete one orbit.

N

NEWTON’S LAW OF UNIVERSAL GRAVITATION

Every mass attracts every other mass.

The force grows with the masses involved and weakens rapidly as the distance between them increases. Newton’s law explains much of planetary motion; Einstein’s general relativity gives a deeper description of gravity, especially in extreme conditions.

EXHIBIT 04 / §4

ATOMIC CORE

How do stars
make energy?

Stars shine because nuclear reactions release energy. In the Sun, nuclear fusion in the core converts hydrogen into helium through a series of reactions.

NUCLEAR FUSION

Small nuclei.
Enormous energy.

The Sun’s core is extremely hot and dense. Under these conditions, hydrogen nuclei can take part in fusion reactions that ultimately produce helium. A small amount of mass is converted into energy.

  3. 03The products have slightly less mass than the starting particles.
  4. 04The mass difference is released as energy, which eventually escapes as sunlight.

Splitting a nucleus

A STAR’S LIFE JOURNEY

One birth. Different endings.

01 / Nebula

A cloud of gas and dust begins to collapse under gravity.

02 / Protostar

The collapsing material heats up as it gathers into a young star.

03 / Main sequence

Stable hydrogen fusion provides energy for a long part of the star’s life.

04 / Later stages

The ending depends on the star’s mass: a Sun like star becomes a white dwarf; a much more massive star may explode as a supernova and leave a neutron star or black hole.

Our Sun is expected to become a red giant, shed its outer layers as a planetary nebula, and leave behind a white dwarf not a supernova.

EXHIBIT 05 / §5

COSMIC FORGE

Where do the
elements come from?

The universe began with mostly hydrogen and helium. Stars transformed matter over time, making many of the elements that build planets and living things.

H
THE EARLY UNIVERSE

Hydrogen & helium

The Big Bang produced mostly hydrogen and helium, with tiny amounts of other light nuclei. These became the raw materials for the first stars.

↓
He
INSIDE STARS

Carbon & oxygen

As stars evolve, helium fusion can build carbon and oxygen. The exact elements formed depend on the star’s mass and stage of life.

↓
Fe
MASSIVE STARS

Elements up to iron

Massive stars can build progressively heavier nuclei through fusion, reaching the iron group. Fusion beyond iron generally does not release energy in the same way.

✳
Au
COSMIC EVENTS

Heavier than iron

Many elements heavier than iron form through neutron capture processes, including in some stellar explosions and neutron star mergers. Ejected material enriches space and can become part of new stars and planets.

✳
We are made of matter with a cosmic history.

The atoms in planets—and in our bodies—carry traces of processes that happened in the early universe and inside generations of stars.

EXHIBIT 06 / §6

UNIVERSAL CURTAIN CALL

How might the universe
come to an end?

The universe’s distant future depends on how expansion evolves, what dark energy is, and how gravity behaves on the largest scales. These are possible scenarios, not confirmed predictions.

POSSIBILITY 01

The Big Freeze

If expansion continues, galaxies become more isolated, star formation eventually dwindles, and usable energy becomes increasingly spread out. The universe grows colder and darker over immense timescales.

A leading scenario in current cosmology

POSSIBILITY 02

The Big Rip

If dark energy grows stronger over time, expansion could eventually overcome the forces holding structures together, potentially tearing apart galaxies, stars, planets, and eventually matter itself.

Requires a particular kind of dark energy

POSSIBILITY 03

The Big Crunch & Big Bounce

If expansion were to reverse, gravity could draw the universe back into a dense state called a Big Crunch. Some speculative models propose a Big Bounce, where contraction is followed by a new expansion.

Not supported as the expected outcome by current observations

THE OPEN QUESTION

Our universe has a history.
Its final chapter is still being studied.

Cosmology changes as new measurements refine our picture of the cosmos. The best scientific answer is the one that follows the evidence and admits what is not yet known.
